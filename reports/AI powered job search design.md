# Make AI drive trustworthy job discovery

Research checked November 1, 2026. Scope: technology jobs worldwide, manual and AI search, no map in the first release, and modular additions later.

**Build one job-search workspace with two equally capable inputs: conversation and ordinary controls.** AI should interpret the user's goals, run real searches, refine them, and explain returned jobs; manual filters should edit the same search. Results belong in persistent job cards, rather than being available only inside a chat transcript. Separate office location from remote work eligibility, and distinguish both from visa sponsorship. Fetch requested provider pages with bounded caching instead of rebuilding a complete local job inventory. Reuse the current Go, React, Postgres, provider adapters and shared AI infrastructure. The recommendations below are product and engineering proposals grounded in the cited documentation and repository review; they are not claims that a specific interface will improve hiring outcomes.

## One workspace should connect conversation to visible criteria

The proposed product promise is straightforward: **describe the work you want, find real openings, understand the tradeoffs, and prepare an application using your chosen resume.** Make a natural-language composer prominent on Discover, with examples such as “Find mid-level Go backend roles I can do remotely from Germany.” Keep title, company, arrangement and location controls visible or one action away. Every interpretation becomes editable criteria above the results. A manual change immediately updates the assistant's search context; a follow-up message updates those same controls. Users should never have to reconcile an AI search with a separate manual catalog.

This direction is supported by existing products, without proving any single layout is best. LinkedIn describes natural-language job queries alongside conventional filters, while acknowledging relevance errors and gaps in some controls. Indeed Career Scout describes follow-up refinements, resume assistance and saved/applied workflows with user decisions. Indeed's search guide separately distinguishes preferences that influence ranking from filters that directly constrain results. Those patterns support combining conversational assistance with inspectable controls; they do not justify removing manual search. ([LinkedIn AI job search](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a6889044), [Indeed Career Scout](https://www.indeed.com/career-advice/finding-a-job/what-is-indeed-career-scout), [Indeed search controls](https://www.indeed.com/career-advice/finding-a-job/tips-on-how-to-get-better-search-results-on-indeed.com))

Start navigation with **Discover and Saved**. Discover contains arrangement choices All, Remote, Hybrid and Onsite; provider names stay in source filters and attribution. On desktop, results receive most of the space, with a compact assistant area and a detail panel. On mobile, Results and Assistant can be switchable views of the same search. Defer a full Applications workspace until its stages and resume associations exist; a destination click alone must not mark a job Applied. Teal demonstrates how tracking can later connect bookmarked jobs, applications and the resume actually used, but that does not make its full workflow necessary for launch. ([Teal application tracking](https://help.tealhq.com/en/articles/14435727-how-to-track-your-job-applications))

Keep three AI activities distinct. **AI search** translates intent and retrieves jobs. **Resume-aware matching** compares an explicitly chosen resume with selected openings. **Resume tailoring** proposes edits to a reviewed copy after a target job is chosen. Someone should be able to search without selecting a resume. Using a resume for fit analysis must not silently authorize edits or permanent preference changes. Manual search should also remain functional when the model is unavailable or its usage limit is reached.

## Work arrangement and eligibility need different meanings

Remote, hybrid and onsite describe work arrangement, not employment type. A contract can be remote and an internship can be hybrid. The normalized model also needs an Unknown arrangement, because source data does not always establish a reliable classification. For Onsite or Hybrid, the location control means office city and country. For Remote, it means **where the candidate plans to work from**, with separate timezone or schedule constraints. With All selected, show the two location concepts explicitly rather than reuse one ambiguous location field.

A worldwide audience is different from a worldwide-eligible remote role. Support searches such as “onsite in London OR hybrid in Berlin OR remote from Spain” as separate location/arrangement branches. Do not accidentally require a single job to satisfy all three. A remote-country match is evidence about the listing's hiring region, not proof of citizenship requirements, sponsorship, employer payroll support or legal work authorization. Unknowns need their own state. An explicit region mismatch should be excluded by default; an unknown restriction can be included through an understandable user choice.

Each card should prioritize title, company, arrangement, office location or remote region, salary with original currency and pay period, and source. Show salary as Not disclosed when missing. Label posted and updated dates correctly, and distinguish source freshness from the original publication date. Details can expose attendance expectations, restrictions, requirements and uncertainties when supplied. Salary comparisons across countries must preserve their original units; a later conversion can be a dated estimate, not a replacement for the source amount. Himalayas explicitly separates country restrictions, timezone restrictions, salary currency and salary period. ([Himalayas data dictionary](https://himalayas.app/docs/data-dictionary))

Do not collapse search relevance, candidate eligibility and resume fit into one percentage. A promising skills match cannot override a country restriction. For fit explanations, show “Relevant evidence,” “Requirements to review” and “Unknowns,” with a job requirement linked to a resume section or bullet. A skill absent from a resume is “not found in this resume,” rather than proof that the person lacks it. Huntr documents requirement and responsibility comparisons with resume evidence; its marketing does not establish a universally calibrated hiring probability. Start with explanations rather than an unexplained 93% score. ([Huntr job match assessment](https://help.huntr.co/en/articles/12241684-job-match-score))

## Data coverage should grow through requested searches

Use the existing sources before adding a large provider roster. **Himalayas should supply targeted remote searches; JSearch should supply broader searches; a small curated company-board registry can add direct employer listings.** Himalayas supports country, keyword, seniority, employment, timezone and page filters. Its search count is an estimate, pages can be short before the end, and results can move between pages. Fetch another page when requested, deduplicate by provider identity, and stop on an empty page rather than treating every short page as final. This supports discovery without claiming an exhaustive local inventory. ([Himalayas API](https://himalayas.app/api))

Provider filters need careful interpretation. JSearch's documented `work_from_home=true` selects remote jobs, but false or omission includes both remote and onsite jobs. It is not an onsite-only switch. Ashby's `isRemote` can cover Remote or Hybrid, so its `workplaceType` is the relevant distinction. Greenhouse and Lever expose employer-specific boards rather than a universal worldwide search service. These differences justify provider adapters with declared capabilities and a shared normalization layer, rather than directly wiring upstream fields into UI filters. ([JSearch documentation](https://www.openwebninja.com/api/jsearch), [Ashby public postings](https://developers.ashbyhq.com/docs/public-job-posting-api), [Greenhouse Job Board API](https://docs.greenhouse.io/job-board), [Lever Postings API](https://github.com/lever/postings-api))

Worldwide tech coverage remains a product goal to measure, not a promise established by API availability. The current registry contains nine company boards, and the English tech-title classifier can miss non-English roles or admit adjacent professions. Retain original titles, language and source categories, then test representative role/country combinations. Add another provider only when that evidence identifies a useful gap. Adzuna's current terms require a commercial licence after its evaluation period, while Jooble's published free-key allowance is lifetime, not monthly; neither should be presented as unlimited free coverage. ([Adzuna API terms](https://developer.adzuna.com/docs/terms_of_service), [Jooble REST API documentation](https://jooblehelpcenter.freshdesk.com/en/support/solutions/articles/60001448238-rest-api-documentation))

Budget for useful searches, refinements and pages, not just monthly users. JSearch currently lists 200 free requests per month and a USD25 monthly plan containing 10,000 requests; active-account pricing and billable units need confirmation before purchase. Our application's fixed prototype allowance is a separate implementation choice. Himalayas publishes higher keyed allowances, but source limits do not remove the need for application budgets. Cache requested public queries/pages across users, coalesce identical work, enforce concurrency and retry limits, and place hard bounds on both cached size and retention. A cache TTL alone cannot bound growth under many distinct queries. ([JSearch pricing](https://www.openwebninja.com/api/jsearch), [Himalayas API reference](https://himalayas.app/docs/remote-jobs-api))

Results should say “Jobs loaded” and “More available,” with understandable partial-result or source-unavailable states. Never sum overlapping provider estimates into an exact worldwide count or label reranking a fetched batch as the best jobs everywhere. Preserve provider cursors and stable result membership for each search session. Exact provider IDs identify records; reliable requisition references or application identities can group cross-source duplicates. Company and title alone are insufficient. Preserve source-specific Apply links and attribution: Himalayas requires its unchanged `applicationLink`, visible credit and removal of expired listings. ([Himalayas API terms](https://himalayas.app/terms))

## A modular Go search engine can serve both inputs

Keep the current application as a **modular monolith**. Separate provider adapters, normalized jobs and eligibility, query orchestration/cache, ranking, user activity, and a small job-assistant package. Reuse `internal/ai` for model construction and the existing Eino tool/streaming patterns. Keep resume mutation tools out of ordinary job search, with an explicit handoff for tailoring. Eino supports schema-defined tool execution and deterministic workflows exposed to conversational agents; a new microservice or vector database is not required merely to make AI central. ([Eino ToolsNode](https://www.cloudwego.io/docs/eino/core_modules/components/tools_node_guide/), [Eino agent and graph guidance](https://www.cloudwego.io/docs/eino/overview/graph_or_agent/))

The shared contract should be a versioned **SearchIntent**: role and skills, explicit arrangement/location branches, seniority, employment type, required versus preferred criteria, salary amount/currency/period, exclusions, sorting and unknown-field choices. Optional resume context belongs to private user state. Record whether criteria came from a manual control, explicit language, or an AI suggestion. Validate this intent in ordinary code, translate it into supported provider queries, normalize returned jobs, apply deterministic eligibility checks, and then rank. AI can suggest a relaxation when strict results are empty; it must not silently apply one.

The current search path reads every available cached job payload into Go, filters and sorts the slice, and only then paginates it. JSearch requests one upstream page and discards continuation state; subsequent UI pages represent cached local listings. These are concrete limitations in [the search service](C:/Users/adity/OneDrive/Desktop/resume-builder/backend/internal/service/job_service.go), [repository](C:/Users/adity/OneDrive/Desktop/resume-builder/backend/internal/repository/job_repo.go), and [provider adapter](C:/Users/adity/OneDrive/Desktop/resume-builder/backend/internal/jobmap/providers.go). They do not establish that 15,000 records has already overloaded production. Replace global inventory scans with query-scoped result membership and repository-side filtering/projection of cached pages. Keep full descriptions out of list responses and ordinary model context. Measure query plans and request costs before choosing indexes or operating caps.

User identity and ownership remain enforced by services, never supplied or authorized by the model. Share generic query results, not raw resumes, conversations or private fit assessments. Treat job descriptions and resume text as untrusted content. Give the assistant narrow tools returning real job IDs and reject invented IDs/links; do not give it arbitrary SQL, URL fetches, shell access or automatic applications. OWASP identifies retrieved-content prompt injection and excessive agency as distinct risks requiring validation and limited privileges, beyond a protective system prompt alone. ([OWASP prompt injection](https://genai.owasp.org/llmrisk/llm01-prompt-injection/), [OWASP excessive agency](https://genai.owasp.org/llmrisk/llm062025-excessive-agency/))

## Build the first release around a complete search task

Consider this illustrative interaction, which makes no claim about available live results: “Find mid-level Go backend roles, remote from Germany or hybrid in Berlin, ideally above EUR70,000 annually.” The assistant shows two editable branches and treats “ideally” as a salary preference. It searches actual providers, presents source-backed cards and marks unknown salary or eligibility. “Only disclosed salaries above that amount” converts the preference into a hard condition. The user can remove that condition manually, and the next message must reflect the updated state. The user then selects a resume, asks why a returned job fits, saves it, and chooses either the original Apply link or “Tailor a copy.” Opening Apply is not evidence of submission.

Implement the contracts, source paging, arrangement-aware controls and typed AI entry point as **one planned first release**. Preserve current bookmarks, source checks and bounded request infrastructure; remove map dependencies from query construction as well as hiding the map. Include real cards/details, visible interpretations, follow-up refinement, Save/Hide and usable manual fallback. The next increment can add chosen-resume snapshots, grounded fit explanations and tailoring handoff. Preserve current local-draft versus saved-resume distinctions so users know which version an assessment used. Rich application tracking, saved-search alerts, persistent preference learning, broader language support and maps can follow independently. Maps should later read optional office coordinates, not redefine remote eligibility.

Validate equivalent manual/AI intent, exclusions and OR branches, country/timezone restrictions, unknown salary/sponsorship, currency periods, duplicate paging, expired jobs, provider outages and prompt-injection attempts. Measure relevance feedback, eligibility violations, duplicates, source coverage, time to first useful saved job, provider calls, cache footprint and model usage. No current evidence supports a specific capacity threshold, cost per user or hiring improvement, so establish baselines before promising targets.

Three product decisions are worth settling during prototyping: should All arrangements be the initial view or should the last explicit choice persist; should unknown salary and eligibility be included with warnings or excluded in strict searches; and should resume-fit analysis launch with discovery or follow immediately afterward? The recommended defaults are transparent arrangement/location controls, no hidden relaxation, and optional selected-resume analysis. Modularity should mean those choices can evolve without replacing the provider contracts or losing saved jobs. The test of this design is whether a user can inspect, correct and act on a real search—not how much conversation the assistant generates.
