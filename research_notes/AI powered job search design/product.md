# Product and UX for AI centered job search

Research date: November 1, 2026. Scope: official product behavior and proposed UX for the existing Go and React resume builder. The user explicitly wants manual search and AI search, proper work arrangement distinctions, and no map for now. The user has now clarified that the first release targets TECHNOLOGY JOBS WORLDWIDE, with modular extensions later. India is an illustrative search country only, not the proposed market boundary. All recommendations below are proposals, not claims that an external product implements them.

## What do existing products establish about conversational and manual job search?

### Takeaway
Verified products combine conversational discovery, ordinary controls, personalized context and downstream application work. The strongest design for this project is one search workspace with two inputs, rather than a chatbot whose recommendations cannot be inspected or a second AI job catalog.

### Cited Findings
- LinkedIn's current help says users can describe desired roles naturally and refine with conventional or suggested filters. It says classic search is being retired, lists currently retained filters, acknowledges that results may miss intent, and names hybrid/onsite and sorting among controls still being restored. It describes semantic title matching and optional personalization from profile/activity. This establishes the pattern and its control tradeoff, not proof that removing classic controls is desirable. Last-updated labels varied between search and opened page, so do not assign an exact update date. — [LinkedIn AI job search help](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a6889044)
- Indeed's article updated October 6, 2026 describes career conversations, follow-up refinements such as remote options or higher pay, resume support, saved/applied jobs in My Jobs, and user review of suggestions. It says the user decides what to submit. Its current article lists desktop and mobile availability, whereas an older regional FAQ says mobile only; avoid blanket global availability claims. Do not reproduce its promotional hire-rate claims as evidence of causal effectiveness for our product. — [Indeed Career Scout](https://www.indeed.com/career-advice/finding-a-job/what-is-indeed-career-scout); [regional FAQ with conflicting availability](https://uk.indeed.com/help/job-seekers/articles/39321745715853-faqs-career-scout?co=GB&hl=en)
- Indeed's October 15, 2026 guide distinguishes preferences used for ranking from filters providing direct result control. It lists pay, arrangement, location, employment type and schedule controls, and job-card preference labels. It describes separate saved searches, feedback and alerts. — [Indeed search guide](https://www.indeed.com/career-advice/finding-a-job/tips-on-how-to-get-better-search-results-on-indeed.com)
- Microsoft's HAX Toolkit describes evidence-based human-AI UX guidance across first use, ongoing use, errors and changes over time, backed by a research synthesis. Use the toolkit to review predictable correction and recovery rather than assuming an AI answer is self-validating. — [Microsoft HAX](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/haxtoolkit/ai-guidelines/)

### Inferences
- Proposed product promise: describe the work you want, get real openings, understand tradeoffs, and prepare a truthful application using your chosen resume. AI should operate the search and assist decisions; it should not become an unverifiable source of jobs.
- Make AI a primary entry point with a prominent prompt composer and useful starters, while a clearly visible Search with filters control opens ordinary title/company and location inputs. Both inputs mutate the SAME structured search state, feed the SAME result list and share bookmarks. Manual search must remain usable without an LLM call.
- Keep three functions visibly distinct: AI search translates a stated intent and refines real search results; resume-based recommendations use an explicitly selected resume to propose roles and prioritize results; resume optimization rewrites a reviewed copy after the user selects a target job. A user can search without a resume, and using a resume for ranking must not imply consent to edit it.
- A chat transcript alone is a poor result format: keep durable, paginated job cards beside a compact assistant conversation. The assistant can explain interpretation, ask one blocking question, refine criteria and compare selected cards. Job descriptions and apply links remain available without engaging chat.
- On desktop, use a results workspace with filters/assistant panel and detail panel; on mobile, make Results and Assistant switchable views of the same search, with details opening separately. Do not squeeze a three-column interface onto a phone. This is a proposal requiring usability validation.
- The first useful AI result should not require a full onboarding questionnaire. Ask the minimum needed to disambiguate a consequential filter: e.g. remote eligibility country versus office city. Offer a resume selector, not a mandatory resume upload.
- Visible search chips should express what the system understood. A sentence like 'Remote Go backend roles, India hiring region, mid level, full time' can map to individually editable controls. Manual chip changes should be reflected in later conversation. Provide Undo for a refinement and a clear Reset search action.
- Treat explicit requirements as hard constraints and preferences as ranking hints. If no strict results exist, say which requirement is blocking and offer a deliberate relaxation. Never silently ignore salary, exclude-US-only, office city or work arrangement because an otherwise strong match appeared.

### Gaps
- Official pages describe features, not independent comparative UX success. No reliable product-comparison trial was found in this limited scope. We cannot claim one layout improves applications or hires by a specific percentage.
- Product availability varies by market/account and official Indeed pages conflict; we should cite the current dated article for behavior without claiming users everywhere can access it.
- No reliable exact competitor UI dimensions or usability results were gathered. Proposed layouts need a prototype and actual task testing.

## How should segregation, result cards and resume explanations work?

### Takeaway
Separate work arrangement, employment type and eligibility rather than letting a location field mean several things. Make fit explanations evidence-based, show unknowns, and preserve user control over the resume used for matching.

### Cited Findings
- LinkedIn says its job-match summaries compare profile/resume qualifications against required and preferred job qualifications. Hirers do not see that match summary. Its labels should be described as product-generated assessments, not a proven probability of an interview. — [LinkedIn job matching](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a7120158)
- Huntr describes a weighted match assessment across qualifications, responsibilities, keywords and role-title meaning, with required items weighted above preferred ones. It presents categories, identifies covered/missing items and links explanations to resume evidence; suggested resume edits can be reviewed. Its claims about applicant competitiveness and interview outcomes are vendor assertions, not independently validated here. Article dated November 13, 2025. — [Huntr Job Match Score](https://help.huntr.co/en/articles/12241684-job-match-score)

### Inferences
- Proposed navigation: Jobs contains Discover, Saved and Applications. Within Discover, use an arrangement selector All, Remote, Hybrid, Onsite. Work arrangement and employment type remain independent: a contract can be remote or onsite; an internship can be hybrid. 'Unknown arrangement' must not be silently treated as onsite. Source names belong in a filter or provenance label, not the primary navigation.
- With no map, results take the main space. Switching to Remote changes the location control label to 'Where will you work from?' and asks for country plus timezone/schedule preferences. Switching to Onsite/Hybrid labels it 'Office location' and supports city/country; hybrid attendance is shown only if supplied. For All, keep office and remote-eligibility controls explicit or show them in separate rows.
- Worldwide scope means location selections should support multiple cities/countries or OR branches rather than assume one fixed home market. Preserve distinct branches such as 'onsite in London OR hybrid in Berlin OR remote from India'. Do not turn this into an impossible AND of all geographies. Store/display salaries in their original currency, period and compensation basis; treat optional converted comparisons as dated estimates, not source facts. A global search must clearly report source coverage gaps and unknowns rather than imply complete worldwide inventory.
- Remote country eligibility is not work authorization, citizenship, visa sponsorship or payroll availability. Do not infer any of these from a country's name, a resume address or an IP location. Candidate work country should be explicit and editable; timezone evidence can be separately unknown or restrictive.
- Proposed job-card order: title and company; arrangement and location/remote country scope; salary with currency and period, or 'Not disclosed'; experience/seniority when reliable; published/updated date correctly labeled; source and freshness; Save, View details and Apply actions. On demand add Why this matches. Show 'India eligible according to listing' or 'Location eligibility unclear', not an unqualified claim of legal employment eligibility.
- Distinguish 'matches your search', 'resume evidence matches the role' and 'you can work from the specified country'. These are three separate assessments. A skill match cannot override an explicit hiring-region mismatch. An absence of resume evidence should be labeled 'Not found in this resume' rather than 'You lack this skill'.
- Use qualitative fit with cited reasons first: 'Relevant evidence', 'Requirements to review', 'Unknowns'. For each claim, reference a job-description requirement and the corresponding resume section or bullet. Avoid invented interview probabilities, unexplained 93% scores, and ATS-pass promises. If a number is later introduced, document what it measures and calibrate it against a labeled dataset.
- Use an explicitly selected resume and snapshot/version for a fit evaluation. Show 'Using Backend resume' with a way to change or remove it. Let users inspect extracted skills and experience. Do not silently promote suggestions into permanent career preferences or edit their resume while they search.
- A direct next step 'Tailor a copy of this resume for this job' fits the existing product. Preserve the original, tie the copy to the job and present proposed edits for review. Do not invent experience, qualifications, work authorization or salary history to raise a score.
- Dates and counts must be truthful. If sources give estimates, page lengths vary or we only searched a bounded number of pages, say 'Results loaded' or 'More available' instead of presenting a local-cache total as worldwide availability. Freshness and source failures should be understandable without exposing internal plumbing in normal flows.
- Empty results need actionable recovery: identify strict filters, offer adjacent titles, widen salary if user permits, include unknown salary explicitly, or include another arrangement. Broadening is an option, never a hidden behavior.
- Suggested illustrative flow (not a claim of live results): user asks 'Find mid-level Go backend jobs I can work remotely from India, preferably at product companies.' AI renders editable criteria: Go/backend, mid level, Remote, work country India; product company is a soft preference because reliable company classification may be unavailable. It searches real sources, returns actual cards and marks unknown salary/eligibility. User says 'Only roles with disclosed pay above 20 lakh INR per year'; AI shows that as a strict condition and does not silently keep unknown-pay listings. User can remove the salary chip manually, ask why a selected job matches, then create a tailored resume copy. No example companies or fabricated result totals are needed.
- Expanded shared-state example: prompt 'Find Go backend roles in London or remote from Germany' becomes visible arrangement branches 'Onsite/Hybrid: London' OR 'Remote work country: Germany', plus role/skill criteria. A follow-up 'Exclude hybrid and contract roles' updates arrangement/employment chips and searches the same result set. User then manually changes Germany to Spain; that same state is updated and the assistant acknowledges Spain next time, rather than reverting to Germany from transcript memory. The details panel and bookmark list retain stable job identities throughout. If provider filtering cannot express a branch, show its retrieval limit and apply an explicit eligibility check; do not silently rewrite the request.

### Gaps
- Whether the current providers reliably support salary, company type, hybrid attendance or experience filters must be established by the provider researcher. Unsupported constraints should be marked as preferences or unavailable, never made up.
- The job database samples discussed earlier show country restrictions separate from office coordinates, but country names/timezones alone do not establish work authorization. A robust eligibility policy needs an explicit definition and test cases.
- No external sources establish a universally calibrated match score. Treat candidate-job fit scoring as our own product design and evaluation problem.

## What workflow should the first release support and what should wait?

### Takeaway
Connect discovery to shortlisting and resume tailoring now; keep full application management and autonomous activity out of the first slice. Success should mean users find credible suitable openings, not that chat produces more text.

### Cited Findings
- Teal's tracking guide describes a pipeline from bookmarked jobs through preparation, applications, interviews and offers, with closed states. It stores notes, dates, contacts and the specific resume used, and allows manually adding a job. This validates a coherent discovery-to-application workflow, not the need to implement its full surface immediately. Page shows July 27, 2026 near the header and March 2026 in the footer; date labeling is internally inconsistent. — [Teal tracking guide](https://help.tealhq.com/en/articles/14435727-how-to-track-your-job-applications)
- Indeed's current Career Scout article joins conversational refinement with My Jobs and reviewed resume improvements, providing an official example of AI assisting multiple steps while users retain decisions. — [Indeed Career Scout](https://www.indeed.com/career-advice/finding-a-job/what-is-indeed-career-scout)

### Inferences
- First release: shared manual/AI search, arrangement-aware location controls, actual-source result cards and details, explicit constraints, paginated on-demand searches, save/hide, basic search history, resume selection, grounded fit explanations on demand, tailoring a resume copy, and original-source Apply links. Include an AI failure path that leaves manual search operational.
- Saved jobs can include an annotation and basic 'Saved' versus 'Applied' state if small. An Apply click should not automatically mark a job Applied; it only proves the user opened the destination. Let users confirm submission. Preserve a job snapshot as permitted by provider terms and link the resume version they actually used.
- Follow-on release: saved searches with bounded alerts, comparison of 2-3 selected jobs, richer application stages/notes, user-controlled preference memory and role exploration. Scheduled alerts need their own budget and notification policy; do not imply they already exist.
- Later: full interview coaching, employer/recruiter networking, email integrations, application autofill, autonomous browsing/application submission, broad salary intelligence and map reintroduction. Maps can later be a secondary office-location visualization, never a remote-eligibility visualization.
- Usefulness evaluation proposals: time to first saved relevant job, the share of reviewed results users mark relevant, hard-constraint violation rate, remote-country false-positive rate, unknown-field rate, duplicate rate, stale/closed apply-link rate, source response latency, provider cost per successful search, AI cost per useful search, and comparison of AI and manual task success. No target percentages are asserted without a baseline.
- Settled scope from the user: technology jobs worldwide, with modular additions later. Remaining product decisions: what resume context AI may use, which constraints are strict, and how to communicate source coverage limits. Proposed defaults: selected resume only, transparent hard constraints, region-aware global results, and reusable modules for providers, search input, results, matching and application workflow. India's use in an example is not an India-first rollout recommendation.

### Gaps
- No user interviews or prototype test has been conducted. The proposed navigation and learning-free entry need quick validation with real search tasks.
- Provider budgets and coverage determine whether a broad career-coach promise is supportable; do not promise exhaustive global inventory before that work.
- Application snapshot retention must follow provider licensing/expiry rules; Teal's own permanent-storage description does not grant this project permission to retain another provider's data permanently.
